## How to use this guide

Professional tests whether an architect can own an enterprise Claude solution from discovery through operation. It has 63 items across seven domains.

| Domain | Weight | Core decision |
|---|---:|---|
| Integration | 19% | Capabilities, identity, latency, observability, RAG, protocols, and progressive discovery |
| Solution Design & Architecture | 17% | Translate business outcomes into proportionate end-to-end patterns |
| Evaluation, Testing & Optimization | 16% | Define evidence, isolate failures, improve safely, and monitor drift |
| Governance, Safety & Risk Management | 14% | Map risk and regulation to enforceable controls, owners, and evidence |
| Stakeholder Communication & Lifecycle | 14% | Discover requirements, defend trade-offs, align SLAs, document, and hand off |
| Models, Prompting & Context Engineering | 13% | Select by measured fit and manage reusable, relevant context |
| Developer Productivity & Operational Enablement | 7% | Make configuration, workflows, debugging, and ownership sustainable for teams |

## Professional answer frame

For each scenario, identify:

1. The business outcome and named constraint.
2. The failing system boundary.
3. The smallest structural correction at that boundary.
4. Evidence that will prove the correction works.
5. Residual risk, responsible owner, and lifecycle implication.

An answer is usually weak when it adds monitoring instead of removing unnecessary privilege, changes a model instead of repairing retrieval, promises every stakeholder goal without negotiating an SLA, or treats compliance as a label rather than a control-and-evidence system.

## Foundations overlap

Foundations mechanics remain relevant, but Professional is broader. Expect to reason about model and protocol selection, RAG data shape, evaluation design, authorization, regulations, business trade-offs, operational readiness, and stakeholder communication. Product trivia without an end-to-end decision is insufficient.
